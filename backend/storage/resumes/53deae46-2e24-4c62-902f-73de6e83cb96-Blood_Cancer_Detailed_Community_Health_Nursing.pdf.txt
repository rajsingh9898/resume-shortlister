BLOOD CANCER
 Detailed Notes – Community Health Nursing | B.Sc. Nursing
 
1. Definition
Blood cancer is a group of cancers affecting the blood, bone marrow, lymphatic system, or blood-forming
cells. The three major types are leukemia, lymphoma and multiple myeloma.
2. Major Types of Blood Cancer
Leukemia: Cancer of blood-forming cells in which abnormal cells, commonly white blood cells, multiply
excessively and interfere with normal blood-cell production.
Lymphoma: Cancer of lymphocytes and the lymphatic system, commonly involving lymph nodes. It includes
Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma.
Multiple myeloma: Cancer of plasma cells in the bone marrow. It may cause bone damage, anemia, infection
and kidney problems.
3. Types of Leukemia
 ALL – Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia
 AML – Acute Myeloid Leukemia
 CLL – Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia
 CML – Chronic Myeloid Leukemia
4. Risk Factors
 Increasing age
 Genetic/chromosomal abnormalities
 Family history in some blood cancers
 Previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy
 Significant radiation exposure
 Certain occupational chemical exposures, such as benzene
 Certain infections associated with specific blood cancers
 Some immune-system disorders
5. Pathophysiology – Simple Flow
Genetic changes/risk factors → abnormal blood-forming cell → uncontrolled multiplication →
abnormal cells occupy bone marrow/lymphatic tissue → normal blood-cell production decreases →
anemia + infection risk + bleeding → tissue/organ involvement.
6. Signs and Symptoms
 Persistent fever or recurrent infections
 Fatigue and weakness
 Pallor due to anemia
 Easy bruising or bleeding
 Nose or gum bleeding
 Unexplained weight loss
 Night sweats
 Painless lymph-node enlargement
 Bone or joint pain
 Loss of appetite

 Recurrent infections
7. Disease-wise Symptoms
Leukemia: fever, infections, anemia, bruising/bleeding, bone pain and lymph-node enlargement.
Lymphoma: painless lymph-node swelling, fever, night sweats, weight loss, fatigue and itching.
Multiple myeloma: bone pain, fractures, anemia, recurrent infections, high calcium and kidney problems.
8. Diagnosis
1. CBC: assesses hemoglobin, WBCs and platelets.
2. Peripheral blood smear: examines blood cells microscopically.
3. Bone marrow aspiration/biopsy: important for diagnosing many leukemias and myeloma.
4. Lymph-node biopsy: used when lymphoma is suspected.
5. Imaging: CT, MRI, PET-CT or X-ray as clinically indicated.
6. Special tests: flow cytometry, cytogenetic and molecular tests.

9. Treatment
Treatment depends on the exact type, stage/risk category and patient's condition.
 Chemotherapy
 Targeted therapy
 Immunotherapy
 Radiotherapy
 Hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation
 Blood or platelet transfusion and other supportive care
10. Community Health Nursing Management
A. Primary Prevention: Promote healthy lifestyle, balanced nutrition and physical activity; discourage
tobacco; educate about occupational safety; reduce unnecessary radiation exposure; and promote awareness
of cancer warning signs.
B. Secondary Prevention: Recognize persistent warning symptoms, encourage early medical consultation,
facilitate appropriate referral, support recommended investigations, and follow up referred patients.
C. Tertiary Prevention: Promote treatment adherence, regular follow-up, infection and bleeding prevention,
adequate nutrition, pain management, rehabilitation, psychological support and family education.
11. Nursing Care at Community Level
Infection prevention: hand hygiene, personal hygiene, safe food/water, avoiding exposure to infectious
persons when immunity is low, temperature monitoring and prompt reporting of fever.
Bleeding prevention: avoid unnecessary trauma, observe for bruising/bleeding, use oral-care measures
advised by the treating team, and promptly report significant bleeding.
Nutrition: balanced nutritious diet, adequate fluids unless restricted, small frequent meals when appetite is
poor, and food-safety precautions.
Psychological care: listen to concerns, provide understandable information, encourage family support and
refer for professional support when needed.
12. Health Education
 Take medicines and treatment exactly as prescribed.
 Do not stop cancer treatment without medical advice.
 Attend regular follow-up visits and investigations.
 Maintain hand hygiene and food safety.
 Avoid tobacco and unnecessary alcohol.
 Take adequate rest and maintain nutrition.
 Report fever, unusual bleeding, severe weakness, breathlessness or sudden deterioration promptly.
13. Role of Community Health Nurse
 Health education
 Risk-factor reduction
 Early recognition of warning signs
 Timely referral
 Treatment-adherence support
 Home visits and follow-up
 Monitoring for complications

 Infection and bleeding-prevention education
 Nutritional and psychosocial support
 Rehabilitation and family education
14. Prevention & Control Flow
Health education → Risk reduction → Early recognition → Timely referral → Diagnosis → Treatment
adherence → Prevention of complications → Rehabilitation → Follow-up.
15. Important Exam Points
 Three major blood cancers: Leukemia, lymphoma, multiple myeloma.
 Common complications: anemia, infection and bleeding.
 Important investigations: CBC, peripheral smear, bone marrow examination and disease-specific tests.
 Community nursing focus: prevention, early recognition, referral, follow-up, treatment adherence,
complication prevention and health education.
Conclusion
Blood cancer requires early recognition, accurate diagnosis and specialized treatment. Community health
nurses play an important role in prevention, health education, early referral, home-based support, treatment
adherence, complication prevention, rehabilitation and family counselling.
